# For researchers

NEURAL1 provides a constrained, inspectable setting for studying model behavior.
Start by deciding what observation would count as success, which control could
disprove your interpretation, and what evidence you need to retain.

## Evidence to inspect before drawing conclusions

Read [Pilot 001](../docs/neural1/research/pilot-001/README.md) with its
[limitations](../docs/neural1/research/pilot-001/limitations.md) and
[negative results](../docs/neural1/research/pilot-001/negative-results.md).
Its incomplete matrix cannot support the planned comparison across all models
and families. The [Pi closeout](../docs/neural1/operations/pi-closeout-2026-09-10.md)
is a separate operational record, not a rewrite of that pilot.

For historical experiments, the
[broad research index](../docs/neural1/history/source-ingestion.md) and
[reviewed console corpus](../docs/neural1/history/console-corpus-review.md)
have different scopes. Preserve missing prices as null and tie historical
claims to reviewed sources.

## Keep the conclusion proportional to the evidence

A model's explanation is not an execution trace. An accepted command is not a
validated scientific hypothesis. A replicated observation is not automatically
a causal result. Use explicit criteria, controls, and retained counterexamples
before widening a claim.

The [research agenda](../docs/neural1/research/research-agenda.md) describes
future questions. The [initial technical report](../docs/neural1/publications/technical-report.md)
records the foundation's methods and limitations; use
[current status](../docs/STATUS.md) for later operational progress.
