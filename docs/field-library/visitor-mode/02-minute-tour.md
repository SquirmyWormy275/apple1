# Two-minute tour

For someone who slowed down on their way past. One idea, one thing to look at,
and a way out.

Words to say are in quotes. Everything else is stage direction.

---

## 0:00 The hook

> "That whole computer runs on software that's 256 bytes long. Not megabytes.
> Bytes. It's about the length of a short paragraph."

Pause. Let that land. It is genuinely surprising and it is true.

## 0:20 What they're looking at

> "This is a Replica 1 Plus. It's a modern reproduction of the Apple-1, the
> computer Steve Wozniak designed in 1976. It's not one of the originals, there
> were only about two hundred of those. This one was built to the same idea,
> decades later."

Say the whole thing. "Modern reproduction" is not a disclaimer you bury; it is
part of what makes it interesting.

## 0:45 The one idea

> "In 1976, if you wanted to use a computer, you flipped switches. Rows of them,
> one bit at a time, and you read the answer off little lights. The thing this
> machine did that was new was let you *type*. Keyboard in, screen out. That's
> it. That's the whole leap."

## 1:15 The thing to look at

Hold up the byte card.

> "Every one of these is just a number. Watch."

Turn it over to show the decoded side.

> "Same numbers. I've just taken 128 off each one and looked them up in a table.
> The computer doesn't know these are letters. It only knows they're numbers.
> Something has to decide what they mean, and that something is the program."

## 1:45 One question back

> "This one's 65. If A is 65, what do you reckon B is?"

They will say 66. They will be right. That is the point.

## 2:00 Where to go next

> "There's a whole set of these written up, starting from nothing. Ask me for
> S01 if you want the one that assumes you know absolutely nothing."

---

## The boundary

Somewhere in the first minute, once they lean in:

> "Look as close as you like. I'd just ask you not to touch the board or the
> cables. We're partway through diagnosing a fault and anything that moves has
> to get written down."

## Off-device fallback

**There isn't one, because nothing above uses the machine.**

The byte card works on a table, in a corridor, in a power cut. If the machine is
present it sits there and gets looked at, which is all a visitor needs from it.
If it is absent entirely, change one word: "this *would be* a Replica 1 Plus" and
carry on.

## The card

Print at 40 columns. Front:

```text
C8  C9  A0  D4  C8  C5  D2  C5
```

Back:

```text
C8 C9 A0 D4 C8 C5 D2 C5
-80 EACH:
48 49 20 54 48 45 52 45
LOOK THEM UP:
H  I     T  H  E  R  E
```

## Sources

- 256-byte monitor: WOZ-FWD p. 17.
- Roughly two hundred originals, 1976, Wozniak's design: H-MADE, H-FOUNDED.
- Keyboard and display as standard input and output, against the Altair's lights
  and switches: H-KBD-STD, H-ALTAIR.
- Replica 1 Plus as a modern product with its own June 2014 manual: BRIEL.
- Byte values and the high-bit convention: A-CHART, P-HIGHBIT.

## What this tour does not establish

That the machine works, in any respect. Nothing in it is a claim about value or
authenticity. It authorizes no hardware action.
