## Part B: sort the statements

| # | Statement | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost $666.66, still had to buy a keyboard | **B** | A price and a bare-board sale describe the 1976 product. |
| 2 | It is 256 bytes long | **M** | Bytes measure a program's length. Hardware is not measured in bytes. |
| 3 | Manufactured decades after 1976, new user manual | **R** | Both facts are about recent manufacture. |
| 4 | Runs the instant you press reset | **M** | Running is something a program does. |
| 5 | About two hundred made in 1976 | **B** | A production count for the original board. |
| 6 | Its ROM also holds BASIC and an assembler | **R** | The Replica 1 Plus ROM holds BASIC and the Krusader assembler alongside the monitor. A 1976 Apple-1 did not ship BASIC in ROM; BASIC came on cassette. |

**The harder one is #4.** Both the original board and the replica start their
monitor at reset, so learners often answer B or R. The statement is still about
the monitor, because the monitor is the thing that runs. The board and the
replica are the things it runs *on*. If a learner argued for B or R and can
state that distinction, they have understood the lesson.

Statement #6 is the common second guess. Accept R with the reasoning above, and
use it to point out that the replica's ROM contains more than the original's
did, which is one concrete way the two machines differ.

## Part C: fix the sentence

1. "This Apple-1 was built in 2014."
   **Fix:** "This Replica 1 Plus was built in 2014."
   *The error:* no Apple-1 was built in 2014. Apple-1s were built in 1976. A
   replica built in 2014 is a replica.

2. "I plugged in the Apple-1 and the monitor came up on the monitor."
   **Fix:** "I plugged in the board, and the monitor program's prompt appeared
   on the display."
   *The error:* one sentence, two meanings of "monitor." Use *monitor program*
   for the software and *display* or *screen* for the hardware.

3. "The Woz Monitor is the chip that the Apple-1 boots from."
   **Fix:** "The Woz Monitor is a program stored in a ROM chip, and the machine
   runs it after reset."
   *The error:* confusing a program with the chip that stores it. The chip is
   the container. The monitor is the contents.

## Part D: sample museum label

> Replica 1 Plus, Briel Computers. A modern reproduction of Wozniak's 1976
> Apple-1 board. New parts, new manufacture, similar software. Not an original.

Twenty-two words. A learner's label is correct if it names the object as a
replica or reproduction, does not present it as a 1976 artifact, and does not
claim anything about condition or working state.

## README: Check your understanding

1. **Two follow-up questions.** Any two of: "Was it made in 1976 or recently?"
   "Who manufactured it, Apple Computer or a replica maker?" "Is it a bare
   board, or does it come with a keyboard and power supply?" "Does its ROM have
   BASIC in it?" The point is that age, maker, and ROM contents each separate an
   original from a replica.

2. **Software, and you can tell from the unit.** Bytes measure a quantity of
   stored information. A physical object is described by its parts and
   dimensions, not by a byte count. Anything measured in bytes is software or
   data.

3. **A keyboard, a power supply, and a video display.** The $666.66 bought the
   assembled circuit board alone.
