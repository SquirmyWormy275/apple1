Keys refer to the shared pool in `../SOURCES.md`. Every date and claim on the
cards is listed here.

## The five cards

| Card | Claim | Key |
|---|---|---|

## Dates are given no more precisely than the sources allow

Four of the five cards carry a year only. Only card B carries a month, because
only that source gives one. The answer key states explicitly that the order of
cards B and C within 1976 is an inference rather than a documented sequence.

This is the H01 brief's requirement, that all dates and claims be cited, applied
strictly: no date in this packet is more precise than its source.

## What these sources cannot date

Recorded because Part E turns it into an exercise. Neither OWAD nor BRIEL, as
indexed for this library, supplies a date for: the Altair 8800's release, the
Apple II's introduction, Bill Gates's Open Letter to Hobbyists, the founding or
first meeting of the Homebrew Computer Club, or the release of Apple's cassette
software.

All five are *mentioned* in the sources. None is dated. The lesson therefore
excludes them from the timeline rather than dating them from general knowledge.

Recorded as **V-28**: this library can order five Apple-1 events from its own
sources and no more. Any wider timeline needs sources this project does not hold.

## A note on tone

The H01 brief and the H02 brief both ask for respect toward collectors. Card E
and Part F discuss a specific person's board and auction. The lesson describes
what the source says he did and draws a general point about survivorship. It makes
no claim about the board's authenticity, condition, or value, and no judgement
about the sale.

## What this lesson does not establish

Nothing about this project's machine, and no claim about the authenticity,
condition, or value of any object. It authorizes no firmware load, EEPROM write,
CFFA1 write, serial-port open, or physical modification.
