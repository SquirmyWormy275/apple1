# H05 Answer key

## Part A: today's entry

Acceptance criteria:

- All six fields filled, including "what I am not sure about." An entry with that
  field blank has not passed; if the learner genuinely has no uncertainty, the
  honest entry is "nothing I noticed," which is itself a claim they may revise.
- "What I saw" contains observations, not conclusions.
- "What I thought it meant" is separate and marked as interpretation.
- The date is today's, not a range.

## Part B: separate the three

| # | Did | Saw | Meant | True in five years |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opened the port | The display garbled | The serial path is broken | **Did and Saw.** The conclusion may not survive; it is one hypothesis among several. |
| 2 | Ran the emulator | It reported returning to the monitor | The program works | **Did and Saw.** "Works" is undefined and, per M03, an emulator result says nothing about hardware. |
| 3 | Pressed reset | A backslash appeared | The ROM is fine | **Did and Saw.** A backslash shows something responded like a monitor. Any compatible image would do the same. |

The pattern: the first two clauses of each sentence survive and the third is an
inference. All three sentences are the S04 error in the shape people actually
write it, which is as a single sentence with "so" in the middle.

**The word "so" is where the observation ends and the conclusion begins.** That is
a useful thing to watch for in your own writing.

## Part C: what is missing

> "Worked on the Apple 1 today. Tried a few things. Seems better now."

Fails every field:

| Field | Missing |
|---|---|
| Date | "Today" is not a date once the page is filed. |
| Who was there | Absent. |
| What I did | "A few things" names nothing. |
| What I saw | Absent entirely. |
| What I thought it meant | "Seems better" is a conclusion with no observation behind it. |
| What I am not sure about | Absent, and "seems" is doing the work an uncertainty field should. |

Worse than the omissions: **"better" implies a change was made and does not say
what.** A future reader cannot tell what state the machine is now in, which means
even the working state is unknown. This is M04's bad bug report as a log entry.

## Part D: the ambiguous gap

1. **Either nothing was done for three weeks, or things were done and not
   written down.** A third possibility: entries were written and lost.
2. **One line, once:** "No work this week." It converts a gap into a record.
3. **A few seconds.** That is the whole cost, and it buys certainty for whoever
   reads the log later, including you.

## Part E: worth logging

| # | Log it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Yes** | It records that the question was asked and answered, so nobody wonders whether it was considered. |
| 2 | **Yes** | The before photograph is the one that gets forgotten and the one you need. |
| 3 | **Yes** | Documents change. Without a version, a future reader cannot reconstruct what you followed. |
| 4 | **Yes** | It removes gap ambiguity for one line. |
| 5 | **Yes** | A wrong guess, recorded, stops the next person making it. An unrecorded one gets made again. |
| 6 | **Yes** | Same question as 5. |

**Items 5 and 6 are the same question and the answer is yes.** A log that only
records successes is a log that will mislead somebody, most likely you. The
mistakes are frequently the most useful entries, and this project's own
display-garbling `STOP` is a good example: it was recorded rather than tidied
away, and it is load-bearing evidence now.

## Part F: read your own old notes

No fixed answer. The usual result is more than expected, and the usual reaction
is mild dismay. Both are the point.

If a learner finds none, they either wrote unusually well or are reading
generously. Suggest they hand the note to somebody else and ask.

## Part G: the entry you would want to find

Common answers: the day the board was acquired, with its condition and where it
came from; the last time anyone saw it working, and what "working" meant; what
was changed by any previous owner.

**The follow-through is the exercise.** Whatever entry you wish existed, its
equivalent for today can be written now, in about two minutes, and in twenty
years it will be the entry somebody wished existed.

## README: Check your understanding

1. **Because observations survive and conclusions often do not.** In five years
   the observation is still evidence and the conclusion may have been superseded.
   Written as one sentence, you can no longer separate them.
2. **Because a gap is ambiguous.** Nothing happening and nobody writing look
   identical afterwards, and one line removes the doubt.
3. **Because documents change.** "Followed A05 as of today" lets a future reader
   find the version you actually used. Without it they read a different text and
   wonder why your account does not match.
