# Open display-history research

These items were **not** resolved by the 2026-08-30 deep-research pass. They are
left open deliberately rather than filled with inference.

## Highest priority

### O-1 — Exact VM-4209 introduction date

**Known:** a surviving specimen is recorded as February 1977. [S1]

**Needed:** Sanyo launch bulletin, dated product catalog, distributor price book,
or factory technical article.

**Best route:** 1977 issues of *Sanyo Technical Review* at the National Diet
Library plus period Sanyo industrial-video catalogs. [R2]

### O-2 — VM-4092 versus VM-4209 product segmentation

**Known:** the models overlap chronologically. [S5]

**Needed:** Sanyo literature listing both models with intended applications,
features, regional variants, and/or prices.

### O-3 — Earliest primary Apple use of VM-4209

**Known:** VM-4209 exists by February 1977; an April 1978 unit survives with
Apple property markings. [S1][S8]

**Needed:** dated Apple photograph, inventory, purchase document, or trade-show
record identifying the model between those bounds.

**Best route:** Stanford M1007 Box 10 Folder 12 and adjacent 1977 material. [R3]

### O-4 — Basis for V&A “Jobs took this Sanyo model to trade fairs” statement

**Known:** the statement appears in a published curatorial interview. [A8]

**Needed:** V&A accession worksheet, curator notes, source photograph, oral
history, or Apple documentation that identifies the model and event.

### O-5 — Byte Shop Sanyo sale evidence

**Known:** a June 1976 VM-4092 has strong original-owner Apple-1/Byte Shop
provenance. [S5]

**Needed:** 1976/77 Byte Shop receipt, price sheet, catalog, correspondence, or
owner photograph naming/showing a Sanyo model.

No such transaction-level document was located in this pass.

## Secondary priorities

### O-6 — Earliest model-specific U.S. VM-4209 advertisement

**Known:** January 1978 BYTE explicitly lists VM4209 at $150. [S7]

The research conversation previously surfaced late-1977 claims, but January
1978 is the earliest model-specific advertisement preserved in the audited
source register unless an earlier issue is independently reverified.

### O-7 — “1977 Apple II advertisement shows VM-4209”

**Status:** `UNVERIFIED`.

The commonly repeated claim has not been tied here to an authenticated primary
Apple advertisement that visibly/explicitly establishes VM-4209. The December
1977 BYTE ad preserved in this repository instead shows a color television and
promotes use of a standard color TV. [A7]

A different 1977 advertisement could still exist. Upgrade this claim only after
the primary page, publication, issue, date, and monitor identification are
recorded.

### O-8 — Official meaning of “Videoscope” for VM-4209

**Status:** `UNVERIFIED`.

Modern seller language is not enough. Resolve using physical unit badging and
period Sanyo literature.

### O-9 — U.S./NTSC VM-4209 service literature

**Known:** an English 625-line / 220 V service-manual scan exists. [S2]

**Needed:** authenticated U.S. 120 V / 60 Hz VM-4209 service manual or regional
supplement. This is a documentation objective only, not a service procedure.

### O-10 — Apple-property VM-4209 asset tags

**Known:** physical Apple markings are documented by auction photography. [S8]

**Needed:** Apple asset ledger, department inventory, employee record, or other
corporate source explaining tags 0027 / 23537 and the monitor's use.

## Completion standard

1. recorded in `sources.md`;
2. classified by source type;
3. checked for internal contradictions;
4. used to update `evidence-ledger.md`;
