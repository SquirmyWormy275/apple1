# Sanyo VM-4209

**Research status:** evidence-reviewed through 2026-08-30.

## Historical classification

The VM-4209 is best described as a late-1970s professional monochrome composite
video/data monitor that entered personal-computer retail channels and later
became strongly associated with Apple-1 display practice in collections and
auctions.

It was **not** an official Apple-1 monitor.

## Earliest dates located

A private collection inventory identifies surviving VM-4209s dated February
1977 and November 1977. The February unit is the earliest physically dated
VM-4209 located in this research. [S1]

This gives a lower bound, not a launch date:

> `CONFIRMED AS SURVIVING-SPECIMEN EVIDENCE`: VM-4209 existed by February 1977.
>
> `UNVERIFIED`: exact Sanyo announcement/launch date.

## Computer-market evidence

Ohio Scientific advertised a complete Challenger computer system with a Sanyo
monitor in May 1977 BYTE, but the model is not named. It is therefore evidence
for Sanyo's participation in the microcomputer-display market, not proof of
VM-4209 use. [S6]

A January 1978 BYTE advertisement from Micro Systems Development explicitly
lists the **Sanyo Monitor (VM4209)** at $150. [S7]

By May 1978, another BYTE ad explicitly offered a new 9-inch VM-4209 for $160
alongside KIM-1, COSMAC VIP, Sol-20, IMSAI, SWTPC and other computer equipment.
[S9]

Later advertising continued to place the model in computer and professional
video markets. A January 1979 BYTE advertisement listed a VM4209 for $109.95.
[S11]

## Technical documentation

An English VM-4209 service manual is preserved at Internet Archive. The
available OCR reports a 625-line, 25-frame-per-second, 220 V / 50 Hz variant,
with high-impedance/75-ohm switchable video input and horizontal resolution
listed above 600 lines. [S2]

This is important for two reasons:

1. A genuine VM-4209 service-manual scan is publicly accessible for research.
2. Its electrical/video standard must **not** be silently applied to every
   VM-4209, especially a U.S. 120 V / 60 Hz unit.

The Henry Ford's U.S. VM-4209 object label, for example, records 120 V, 60 Hz,
32 W and a June 1979 manufacture date. [A4]

The service manual is linked rather than vendored because its online
availability does not establish a redistribution license. It is also **not** a
repair authorization; CRT servicing involves hazardous stored voltages.

## Apple corporate artifact

RR Auction documented an April 1978 VM-4209 bearing multiple Apple property
markings and asset tags. That is strong physical provenance linking a VM-4209
to Apple's corporate environment. The evidence does not establish its exact
acquisition date, department, or use with an Apple-1. [S8]

## Apple-1 collector association

The Apple-1 Registry says famous auctions repeatedly displayed VM-4209/VM-4509
monitors with Apple-1s and specifically warns that this caused people to assume
they were the uniquely correct Apple-1 display. [A2]

Christie's has sold a curated Apple-1 collection containing a working VM-4209
that was tested with the computer. This is genuine later association and
compatibility evidence, not proof of an original 1976 purchase. [A5]

## Claims not yet proven

- No 1976-dated VM-4209 has been authenticated in the sources reviewed here.
- No original 1976 Apple invoice or Byte Shop receipt naming VM-4209 has been
  located.
- No primary Apple document reviewed here calls VM-4209 the Apple-1 monitor.
- The repeated claim that a VM-4209 appears in a 1977 Apple II advertisement
  remains unverified pending identification of the actual primary ad.
- The exact VM-4092/VM-4209 commercial relationship remains unresolved.
- “Videoscope” is not treated as an official Sanyo product-line name without
  primary Sanyo documentation or direct inspection of a physical badge.
