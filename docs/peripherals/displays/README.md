# Apple-1 display and Sanyo monitor research

This directory separates three questions that are often collapsed into one:

1. What display hardware did Apple actually document for the Apple-1 in 1976?
2. What is the documented history of Sanyo's VM-4092 and VM-4209 monitors?
3. How did the VM-4209 later become visually associated with Apple-1 collections,
   auctions, and exhibitions?

The short answer is that Apple did **not** specify or supply a dedicated Apple-1
monitor. The 1976 Apple-1 Operation Manual describes a general composite-video
interface, names Motorola and Ball as example monitor manufacturers, and explains
using a household television through an RF modulator. The Sanyo VM-4209 is a
real early personal-computing/data-display monitor, documented by surviving
specimens in 1977 and in computer-market advertising by early 1978. A surviving
April 1978 VM-4209 carries Apple property markings. None of that establishes that
Apple supplied VM-4209s with Apple-1 computers in 1976.

## Evidence labels

Every consequential claim should be understandable without guessing how strong
its support is.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CONFIRMED-PRIMARY` | Direct contemporary document or physical-object record supports the claim. |
| `CONFIRMED-INSTITUTIONAL` | Museum/archive catalog record directly supports the claim. |
| `STRONG-PROVENANCE` | Specific surviving artifact or original-owner chain supports it, but a contemporary transaction document is absent. |
| `SECONDARY` | Later scholarly, collector, auction, or institutional narrative. Useful, but not primary evidence. |
| `INFERENCE` | Reasoned from cited evidence and explicitly identified as inference. |
| `UNVERIFIED` | Plausible or repeated claim for which the needed primary evidence has not been located. |
| `REJECTED` | Evidence reviewed contradicts the claim or its cited support does not establish it. |

## Boundaries

This research documents history and provenance. It does not authorize powering,
opening, servicing, recapping, probing, modifying, or connecting any vintage CRT.
CRT monitors can retain dangerous voltages after power is removed. Technical
manuals are linked for historical reference only.

External photographs and scans are not copied into this repository unless their
reuse status is recorded in [image-rights-ledger.md](image-rights-ledger.md).
Where rights are unclear or restricted, the repository links to the source rather
than republishing the image.
