# Visual chronology: Apple-1 displays and the later Sanyo canon

This page uses only images whose reuse status was reviewed. It deliberately
**does not embed a Sanyo VM-4092/VM-4209 photograph** because the strongest
historical Sanyo images located in this research are museum, auction, forum, or
seller photographs without a repository-reuse grant. Those sources are linked
below instead.

The public-domain and Creative Commons images below are embedded from their
canonical Wikimedia file URLs rather than duplicated in this repository. See
[image-rights-ledger.md](image-rights-ledger.md) for the licensing review.

## 1976 — Apple sells a motherboard with a video terminal, not a monitor bundle

![Apple-1 advertisement, October 1976](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/48/Apple_1_Advertisement_Oct_1976.jpg)

**Apple-1 advertisement, October 1976.** Apple Computer Company; scan by Michael
Holley from *Interface Age*. Wikimedia Commons records the advertisement as
public domain in the United States because it lacked a separate copyright
notice. [A9]

**What it establishes:** Apple's contemporary pitch emphasized the board's
built-in video-terminal electronics and said a video monitor or home TV could
be used.

**What it does not establish:** a particular monitor brand or model.

## A surviving Apple-1 board

![Apple-1 at the Computer History Museum](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/18/Apple-1%2C_1976%2C_Computer_History_Museum.jpg)

## 1977 — Apple II advertising foregrounds the consumer color television

![Apple II advertisement, December 1977, page 1](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c0/Apple_II_advertisement_Dec_1977_page_1.jpg)

![Apple II advertisement, December 1977, page 2](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4a/Apple_II_advertisement_Dec_1977_page_2.jpg)

**Apple II advertisement, December 1977 BYTE.** Apple Computer Inc.; scans on
Wikimedia Commons. Commons records both advertisements as public domain in the
United States. [A7]

Page 2 explicitly tells the reader to bring in the color TV and says Apple II
plugs into a standard color television. This is useful negative evidence when
evaluating the often-repeated claim that a VM-4209 was *the* display promoted
with the Apple II in 1977.

It does **not** prove Apple never used monochrome Sanyo monitors internally or
at another event.

## Later museum ensemble — date labels defeat visual assumptions

The Henry Ford's Apple-1 ensemble includes a Sanyo VM-4209 whose rear label is
transcribed by the museum as **manufactured June 1979**. [A4]

That is valuable even without republishing the museum photograph: the monitor
is genuinely part of the current ensemble but physically cannot be an original
1976 pairing. This is a concrete warning against reading historical chronology
from a modern display photograph alone.
